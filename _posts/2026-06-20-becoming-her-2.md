Becoming Her 2.0 ran in June with 90+ people on the call. I want to write down what worked, what didn't, and what I'd tell anyone trying to build something similar from scratch.

## What Becoming Her is

It's a webinar series I run under IDENA — a brand I started specifically for this work. The focus is women in engineering in Nigeria: not inspirational talks, but practical ones. How do you get an internship when you have no network? What does a research portfolio look like at year two? How do you navigate being the only woman in a hardware lab?

I started it because I was looking for this kind of content and couldn't find it. Not in the form I wanted. There are big conferences and mentorship initiatives, but a 90-minute live session with Q&A where you can actually ask things — that was missing.

## The logistics of 90 people

I built the registration web app from scratch. I also built a raffle tool for the giveaway at the end, because I wanted it integrated with the registrant list rather than a generic third-party wheel. BCC email campaigns to 64+ registrants. The whole thing ran without an external events platform.

This matters because the ops side of an event is where passion projects die. If the registration flow is slow or the confirmation email doesn't arrive, people don't show up. I'd estimate I spent about equal time on the content and on the infrastructure.

What I'd do differently: automate the follow-up email sequence earlier. I sent reminders manually. That doesn't scale past ~50 people.

## The conversation itself

The session was about building a technical career as a woman engineer in Nigeria — backend paths, hardware paths, research. What actually surprised me was where the questions went. People didn't ask much about technical skills. They asked about confidence. About whether it was normal to feel like the only person in a room who didn't belong. About how I dealt with professors who directed questions to the men.

I wasn't fully prepared for that. I answered honestly, which meant admitting that I'm still figuring it out. I don't know whether that was the right call or the wrong one. But I do know that pretending to have things resolved that aren't resolved is a bad use of a platform.

## Why I'm running a third one

Because 90 people showed up. Because the questions kept coming 45 minutes after I said we'd wrap. Because I got messages afterwards from people at four different Nigerian universities saying they'd been looking for something exactly like this.

We're in planning for edition three. The format will evolve — I want to bring in more guests, more structured workshops. But the core stays the same: real people, real questions, and no pretending this is easier than it is.
