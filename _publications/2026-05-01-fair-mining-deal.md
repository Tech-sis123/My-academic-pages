Fair Mining Deal is a pre-signing evaluation instrument for natural resource agreements between Russian companies and African states. The Fair Mining Deal Index (FMDI) is computed bilaterally — from both host-state and investor perspectives — across seven weighted blocks: economic benefit distribution, localisation, technology transfer, employment, environmental obligations, transparency, and risk balance. Block weights are derived from two public, enumerable sources (31 African mining regulatory measures and the Fraser Institute Annual Survey of Mining Companies 2025) using a Laplace-smoothed revealed-preference method, making the entire assessment reproducible by third parties. Fairness is defined as symmetry: the absolute gap |Δ| = FMDI\_Host − FMDI\_Investor is interpreted as renegotiation risk. Retrospective validation on the 2005 Liberia–Mittal Steel agreement (|Δ| = 60.0 → renegotiated within one year; post-amendment |Δ| = 22.3) correctly classified the outcome across 100% of 20,000 weighted bootstrap draws.

*Co-authored with Sofia A. Afanasieva (team lead) and Polina S. Antonyuk, Peter the Great St. Petersburg Polytechnic University. Esabu Blessing contributed as African Regional Specialist, Expert Panel: African mining law and local context; coding and validation of the host-state corpus.*
