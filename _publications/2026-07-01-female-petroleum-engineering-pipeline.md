Women represent fewer than 10% of practising petroleum engineers in Africa despite comprising roughly half the continent's population. This paper develops a novel dual-stage machine learning framework to address the "leaky pipeline" phenomenon. Stage 1 predicts academic dropout risk among female petroleum engineering students (N = 1,247 records, 28 features; top predictors: financial constraint, female mentorship access, harassment experience). Stage 2 forecasts career exit probability among female graduates (N = 834 alumni records, 32 features; top predictors: workplace inclusion index, maternity/parental policy, professional development access). Ensemble models — Random Forest, XGBoost, and Neural Networks — are trained with SMOTE oversampling; SHAP values provide both global feature rankings and individual explanations for actionable intervention. XGBoost achieves AUC-ROC of 0.921 (Stage 1) and 0.908 (Stage 2). The framework synthesises lessons from international programmes (Athena Swan, NSF ADVANCE, Norway's Ada Project, IEEE WIE STAR) and proposes context-specific adaptations for the Nigerian and broader African petroleum sector.
