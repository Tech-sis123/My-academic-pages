I'm a Computer Engineering student at the University of Lagos. I'm an applied researcher — I build and publish ML systems and computational infrastructure for populations that most research overlooks. I publish under the name **Tech Sis**.

My work follows a consistent question: how does computing need to change when you design it for African contexts, low-resource environments, and structurally underrepresented populations?

**Equity in data-driven systems.** My SPE STSE 2026 paper built a dual-stage ML framework — XGBoost, Random Forest, Neural Network with SHAP-weighted explainability — for predicting female attrition across the petroleum engineering pipeline, trained on 2,081 records. *Fair Mining Deal*, presented at the Russian-African Forum of Young Scientists 2026 (under UNESCO auspices, Saint Petersburg Mining University), builds a bilateral pre-signing fairness index for Russian-African subsoil agreements — the first instrument to score the same draft clause-by-clause from both the host state's and investor's perspectives simultaneously, interpreting the asymmetry gap as renegotiation risk. Retrospectively validated on the 2005 Liberia–Mittal Steel deal: Δ = 60.0 flagged high revision risk; the agreement was renegotiated within a year. I contributed as African Regional Specialist on the Expert Panel — African mining law and local context, and coding and validation of the host-state corpus. Both papers apply reproducible, interpretable methods to surface structural mechanisms, not just describe outcomes.

**Low-resource and African-context systems.** VoiceIQ is an NLP pipeline for Nigerian English and Pidgin code-switching — built because standard English models fail on how most Nigerians actually speak. EthnoLedger, presented at DeSci Lagos 2025, routes indigenous knowledge preservation through USSD and a blockchain backend — not as a novel architecture choice, but because USSD is what works in low-connectivity Nigerian communities. My generator predictive maintenance system applies FFT acoustic analysis for remaining useful life prediction and deploys in four languages (English, Igbo, Yoruba, Hausa), because accessibility is part of the research problem, not an afterthought.

**Systems and hardware infrastructure.** An FPGA-based ECDSA signing engine hardened against side-channel attacks via Montgomery ladder scalar multiplication addresses digital financial security for markets where crypto adoption is outrunning hardware security tooling. For my Signals and Systems research, I built a C++/MATLAB computational framework for adaptive thermoelectric energy harvesting, showing that dynamically switching across DOS-engineered element configurations outperforms fixed-configuration systems — a relevant problem for off-grid energy in sub-Saharan Africa.

I'm currently a SIWES intern at MTN Nigeria's IT Division. Outside research, I lead product and engineering at Elite Global AI (EGIT), where I'm building an antenatal care platform piloting at UBTH. I founded Unilag Female Engineers '29 and chair the IEEE RAS UNILAG student chapter. I run *Becoming Her*, a webinar series hosted by Unilag Female Engineers '29 that has reached 90+ women across Nigeria — because who gets to do research is itself a research question.
