I'm a Computer Engineering student at the University of Lagos (Class of 2029). I'm an applied researcher — I build and publish ML systems and computational infrastructure for populations that most research overlooks. I publish under the name **Tech Sis**.

My work follows a consistent question: how does computing need to change when you design it for African contexts, low-resource environments, and structurally underrepresented populations?

**Equity in data-driven systems.** My SPE STSE 2026 paper built a dual-stage ML framework — XGBoost, Random Forest, Neural Network with SHAP-weighted explainability — for predicting female attrition across the petroleum engineering pipeline, trained on 2,081 records. Through a research collaboration with Peter the Great St. Petersburg Polytechnic University, I've also worked on fairness analysis of Russian-African mining investment agreements, examining how deal structures encode structural disadvantage. Both papers apply interpretable ML to surface mechanisms, not just describe outcomes.

**Low-resource and African-context systems.** VoiceIQ is an NLP pipeline for Nigerian English and Pidgin code-switching — built because standard English models fail on how most Nigerians actually speak. EthnoLedger, presented at DeSci Lagos 2025, routes indigenous knowledge preservation through USSD and a blockchain backend — not as a novel architecture choice, but because USSD is what works in low-connectivity Nigerian communities. My generator predictive maintenance system applies FFT acoustic analysis for remaining useful life prediction and deploys in four languages (English, Igbo, Yoruba, Hausa), because accessibility is part of the research problem, not an afterthought.

**Systems and hardware infrastructure.** An FPGA-based ECDSA signing engine hardened against side-channel attacks via Montgomery ladder scalar multiplication addresses digital financial security for markets where crypto adoption is outrunning hardware security tooling. For my Signals and Systems research, I built a C++/MATLAB computational framework for adaptive thermoelectric energy harvesting, showing that dynamically switching across DOS-engineered element configurations outperforms fixed-configuration systems — a relevant problem for off-grid energy in sub-Saharan Africa.

I'm currently a SIWES intern at MTN Nigeria's IT Division. Outside research, I lead product and engineering at Elite Global AI (EGIT), where I'm building an antenatal care platform piloting at UBTH. I founded Unilag Female Engineers '29 and chair the IEEE RAS UNILAG student chapter. I run *Becoming Her*, a webinar series under my IDENA brand that has reached 90+ engineers across Nigeria — because who gets to do research is itself a research question.
