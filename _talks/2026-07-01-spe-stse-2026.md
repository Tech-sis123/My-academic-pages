Presented paper SPE-STSE-2026-050 at the SPE Student and Technical Symposium and Exhibition 2026. The talk covered a dual-stage machine learning framework for predicting female dropout from petroleum engineering programmes (Stage 1) and career exit among female graduates (Stage 2), using ensemble models (XGBoost, Random Forest, Neural Networks) trained on 2,081 records from Nigerian petroleum engineering programmes and industry. SHAP values provided actionable, individual-level explanations for each prediction.

Key finding: financial constraint, access to female mentors, and workplace inclusion were the dominant risk factors at each stage. The talk proposed context-specific adaptations of international retention programmes (Athena Swan, NSF ADVANCE) for the Nigerian petroleum sector.
